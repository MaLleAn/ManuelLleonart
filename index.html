Research

  • Job‑Market Paper: “Optimal Monitoring Hierarchies under Collusion”

    This paper explores how organizational structure influences the effectiveness of monitoring in firms, particularly under collusion between agents and supervisors. It develops a model with multiple supervisors per agent, tying compensation to aggregated reports to reduce collusion. Extending the principal‑supervisor‑agent framework with a continuum of agents and private productivity, we analyze how supervision intensity, detection likelihood heterogeneity, and cost trade‑offs shape optimal monitoring schemes. The results offer insights into supervisory wage structures and industry monitoring practices.

  • “Feed for Good? On Regulating Social Media Platforms” (with Miguel Risco ) – PDF

    We build a theoretical model of communication and learning on a social media platform, then characterize the engagement‑maximizing algorithm it uses. We show this algorithm overexploits similarity between users, locking them in echo chambers and hampering learning as platform size grows. Counterfactual “breaking‑echo‑chambers” algorithms are proposed to boost learning, and we evaluate horizontal interoperability as a regulatory leverage aligning platform algorithmic design with social welfare.

  • “A Model of Punitive Voting” – PDF (Submitted)

    This paper analyzes a two-period model of political competition where voters care about candidate integrity. Candidates trade off between their own preferred policy and adherence to electoral promises. Voters punish deviation by switching their vote. We find that punitive voting is effective only under low voter uncertainty about preferences; then, candidates issue compromised promises and follow through when elected. We also show that when one candidate’s ideal point is closer to the median voter, only one candidate may face electoral discipline in equilibrium.
